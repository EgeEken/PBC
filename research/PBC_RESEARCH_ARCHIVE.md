# PBC research archive — through Pass 47

Status: research paused. This is a navigation and synthesis document; individual reports and their compact evidence remain authoritative. Historical reports have not been rewritten. The released product remains PBC V3.1; no V4 release was produced.

## Project lineage

PBC began as a procedural image representation in which short brush instructions influence many pixels. V1 searched random placement/size/multiplier combinations; V2 reduced search cost and divided patches into quadrants; V2.3 added a low-resolution image initialization before residual brushes. That initialization was an important rate-distortion improvement on smooth photographic content. V2.4 rebuilt and optimized the code with approximately unchanged quality. V3 replaced guided random placement with iterative residual patches, a learned encoder policy for action parameters, an algorithmic decoder, and rate-aware patch selection. V3.1 retained the V3 stream and semantics while reducing encode/decode overhead.

The later research has two distinct goals: (1) preserving and improving the shipped V3.1 implementation and its measurement quality, and (2) discovering whether V4 needs a new representation to escape V3.1's quality ceiling. Results from an isolated prototype, an ablation, or a local oracle are not a release improvement. The relevant rate is completed-file bytes after the stream's LZMA wrapper. Comparisons require reconstructed source-resolution RGB, declared preprocessing, matched actual rates, and runtime boundaries that are comparable.

## Research stages and conclusions

### V3.1 diagnosis, optimization and packaging (Passes 1–16)

The first passes reconstructed which code and model actually produced benchmark results, established frozen release/runtime comparisons, diagnosed RNG and benchmark-path differences, and separated exact-output implementation work from algorithm changes. Early attribution showed that older reference/runtime names could conceal different code; a “current with a feature off” control is not the released reference. Detailed checks found exact speed opportunities and RD hypotheses, but each was evaluated separately and did not justify combining unrelated controls.

- **Pass 1 — baseline audit and attribution.** Compared immutable release, historical runtime, root tree, and HF observations; captured streams, model/source identities, preprocessing and RGB reconstruction. The canonical reference was chosen over assumptions based on branch names. The HF saved-output discrepancy was not fully explained. See [first-pass results](../PBC_FIRST_PASS_RESULTS.md), [baseline audit](../PBC_BASELINE_AUDIT.md), and [Pass 1 README](../research_pass1/README.md).
- **Pass 2 — RNG, speed and quality frontier.** Tested exact-output performance kernels, stream/entropy transformations, and bounded quality operating points with main/holdout controls. Runtime changes were assessed for output identity; entropy and quality variants did not yield a general release result. A saved HF Quality discrepancy remained unresolved. See [Pass 2 summary](../research_pass2/PBC_PASS2_SUMMARY.md).
- **Passes 3–4 — exact reuse/performance and rate-aware allocation.** Exact-output optimizations and search/error-map reuse were isolated from an allocation-weight study. A weight around 0.4 showed content-dependent RD promise but did not establish a general high-quality frontier or end-to-end release win; broader cache ideas did not repay their complexity. See [Pass 3](../research_pass3/PBC_PASS3_SUMMARY.md), [Pass 4](../research_pass4/PBC_PASS4_SUMMARY.md).
- **Passes 5–10 — HF runtime validation and integration.** Benchmarked controlled optimizations on the actual Space, isolated promotion files, validated integrated runtime behavior, palette/stop-policy changes, and restored release-compatible RNG defaults. Some semantics-preserving speed changes were accepted into the V3.1 line. Palette or selection changes were not supported as general RD improvements. Reports are under [Pass 5](../research_pass5/), …, [Pass 10](../research_pass10/).
- **Pass 11 — timing and final RGB objective audit.** Reconciled a speed discrepancy to sample composition and timer scope rather than a demonstrated codec regression. Found a rare working-resolution vs final-RGB action-order disagreement; it did not establish a beneficial alternate action. See [summary](../research_pass11/PBC_PASS11_SUMMARY.md).
- **Pass 12 — RGB coding ablation.** RGB encoding reduced MSE in some cases but raised complete bitrate substantially (all 16 points used more bits) and did not pass matched-rate RD; YCbCr remained the default. See [results](../research_pass12/PBC_COLOR_SPACE_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 13 — cross-channel patch reuse feasibility.** Spatial overlap/correlation did not support useful shared-patch geometry. Local and saved HF trajectories disagreed in several cases without identifying the HF process-level cause. See [summary](../research_pass13/PBC_PASS13_SUMMARY.md).
- **Pass 14 — native-runtime audit.** Pillow bicubic final resizing dominated a measured decode slice; a Rust prototype was slower and not pixel-exact. A standalone Python package was assembled, not a native decoder replacement. See [summary](../research_pass14/PBC_PASS14_SUMMARY.md).
- **Passes 15–16 — exact performance work.** Fused integral images and bounded integer feature histograms produced semantics-preserving encode gains on controlled comparisons; pass 15 streams were identical and pass 16 model vectors/actions matched. These are implementation improvements, not new RD representation gains. See [Pass 15](../research_pass15/PBC_PASS15_SUMMARY.md), [Pass 16](../research_pass16/PBC_PASS16_SUMMARY.md).

### First V4 representation investigations (Passes 17–28)

- **Pass 17 — full-image DCT residual sidecar.** A reduced PBC base plus signed 8×8 DCT residual layer produced completed-file RD wins on four of six selected images, but Cat and Flower were too expensive. It used a custom P17D container, not a deployable V3 stream change. See [results](../research_pass17/PBC_RD_PROTOTYPE_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 18 — low-bit actions and deterministic spatial proposals.** Replayed canonical candidate pools and tested contrast/binary-fit proposals. Same-region alternative actions were uncommon; the spatial proposal rule did not find a useful replacement. This did not prove stochastic search optimal. See [spatial results](../research_pass18/PBC_SPATIAL_PROPOSAL_RESULTS.md), [summary](../research_pass18/PBC_PASS18_SUMMARY.md).
- **Pass 19 — explicit signed ternary hard masks.** One Googlewiki region locally beat a native grid on gain/packet bytes, but only one of 40 sampled regions passed the material screen; completed files were not justified. Native grids generally matched or beat the mask. See [summary](../research_pass19/PBC_PASS19_SUMMARY.md).
- **Pass 20 — multiscale residual layer.** Coarse/fine residual bands created complete files, but 9/12 were dominated by frozen full-DCT results and the remainder by actual repacks; enhancement entropy could be very costly. See [results](../research_pass20/PBC_MULTISCALE_RESIDUAL_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 21 — sparse coefficient serialization.** Zero-run/zigzag storage preserved coefficients and decoded pixels while bringing payload size near extreme compression with far lower entropy cost. It retained four Pass 17 dominance points and removed the extreme XZ encode bottleneck, but decode and large-photo rate/runtime were unsuitable as a general replacement. See [hybrid results](../research_pass21/PBC_SPARSE_HYBRID_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 22 — sparse decoder batching/reuse.** Batched IDCT and reused buffers preserved exact pixels and reduced allocation cost. Photographic dense work remained expensive; this was experimental decoder evidence, not a production architecture. See [results](../research_pass22/PBC_DECODER_RECONSTRUCTION_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 23 — decoder-aware target preconditioning.** A transformed target improved offline working reconstruction but changed patch trajectories and failed the completed-file progression gate; gains were narrow/traded for rate. See [results](../research_pass23/PBC_DECODER_AWARE_RD_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 24 — native patch omission.** In 80 selected patch omissions, seven lowered RGB MSE and only three reduced final LZMA bytes; byte savings were 14–97 B (0.048–0.161%) with tiny MSE changes. No useful pruning rule followed. See [results](../research_pass24/PBC_FINAL_PATCH_REDUNDANCY.md).
- **Pass 25 — transform initialization plus procedural patches.** Standalone initializer capacity was promising, but the completed transform-plus-PBC architecture did not reliably improve RD and JPEG dominated five of eight tested points. See [results](../research_pass25/PBC_TRANSFORM_BASE_RD_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 26 — PBC 3.2 packaging/native metric/video.** Standalone packaging and exact metric work succeeded; temporal video rate efficiency failed. No new codec architecture or user release followed. See [summary](../research_pass26/PBC_PASS26_SUMMARY.md).
- **Pass 27 — 3.2.0rc1 engineering acceptance.** Source/wheel installs, permissions, path handling and package artifacts were tested. It was a local engineering candidate with remaining Windows VM, licensing, signing and user validation gaps; it was not published. See [summary](../research_pass27/PBC_PASS27_SUMMARY.md).
- **Pass 28 — distribution/release acceptance.** Windows distribution and compatibility evidence were assembled for an RC, with reproducible package manifests. The RC is not the current release and does not replace V3.1. See [acceptance](../research_pass28/PBC32_RELEASE_ACCEPTANCE.md), [readiness](../research_pass28/PBC32_RELEASE_READINESS.md), and [reproduction](../research_pass28/REPRODUCE.md).

### V4 encoding and validation research (Passes 29–34)

- **Pass 29 — nonlocal distribution grouping.** Grouped shared residual actions were measured on fixed bases; this was a component study without complete V4 stream evidence. See [results](../research_pass29/PBC_V4_NONLOCAL_GROUPING_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 30 — shared-action coding.** A four-pattern fit cleared the residual-SSE screen only on Cat; other classes were near-zero or small, and an independent-cell control had higher SSE gain but unknown payload cost. No complete stream was built. See [results](../research_pass30/PBC_V4_SHARED_ACTION_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 31 — fixed-grid predictive refinement.** Complete enhancement files decoded correctly; detail could improve reconstruction at substantial rate, but the mode set did not approach selected AVIF points and general RD gains were weak. See [results](../research_pass31/PBC_V4_PREDICTIVE_REFINEMENT_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 32 — rate-aware prediction/generalization.** Held-out mode gains were small (largest about 0.76% MSE at close rate); proposed ranking did not materially improve matched-rate quality. See [results](../research_pass32/PBC_V4_RATE_AWARE_PREDICTION_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 33 — sparse original-resolution transforms.** Sparse 8×8 RGB DCT created useful complete-file gains on selected graphics/photo points, but utility was content-dependent and analysis/encoding expensive. See [results](../research_pass33/PBC_V4_SPARSE_DETAIL_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 34 — sparse transform validation/frequency ablation.** Broader validation exposed the cost and narrow conditions of sparse detail; source-resolution residual transforms did not solve base rate/runtime. AVIF comparison is in [Pass 34 report](../research_pass34/REPORT.md), compact measurements in [results JSON](../research_pass34/results_summary.json).

### AV1/libaom investigation (Passes 35–41)

All AV1 work used pinned libaom 3.12.1 commit `10aece4157eb79315da205f39e19bf6ab3ee30d0` and libavif 1.3.0 commit `1aadfad932c98c069a1204261b1856f81f3bc199`, isolated source exports, one encoder thread and completed AVIF files. It was competitive-reference research, not PBC implementation work.

- **Pass 35 — source/baseline foundation.** Built pinned AVIF and measured unmodified qcolor operating points; documented sources and build recipe. See [baseline](../research_pass35/AVIF_BASELINE_RESULTS.md), [source map](../research_pass35/AV1_ENCODER_SOURCE_MAP.md), [reproduction](../research_pass35/BUILD_AND_REPRODUCTION.md).
- **Pass 36 — profiling/computation reuse.** Profiled native encoder work and repeated computations. See [results](../research_pass36/PASS36_RESULTS.md), [reuse candidates](../research_pass36/COMPUTATION_REUSE_CANDIDATES.md).
- **Pass 37 — intra mode ordering.** Syntax-cost mode-order change did not establish a material completed-file speed improvement. See [results](../research_pass37/PASS37_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 38 — transform-search pruning.** Shadow diagnostics and expanded search measured mispruning but extra search costs outweighed usable benefit; no practical AV1 improvement was established. See [diagnostics](../research_pass38/PRUNING_DIAGNOSTIC_RESULTS.md), [results](../research_pass38/PASS38_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 39 — AQ/perceptual tuning.** Existing AQ/tune configurations were assessed on completed AVIFs using RGB MSE and libjxl SSIMULACRA2. Same-q quality often cost size; near-rate results were mixed. See [results](../research_pass39/PASS39_RESULTS.md), [AQ table](../research_pass39/AQ_RATE_DISTORTION_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 40 — spatial delta-Q allocation.** Variance boost lowered MSE at equal q but incurred substantial rate; after rate calibration the effect was mixed. See [results](../research_pass40/PASS40_RESULTS.md), [ablation](../research_pass40/COMPONENT_ABLATION_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 41 — luma/chroma allocation.** Existing chroma delta-Q was tested at near-matched rates; effects varied and did not establish a broad win. No AV1 code was integrated into PBC. See [results](../research_pass41/PASS41_RESULTS.md).

### First multiresolution V4-A and closure (Passes 42–47)

- **Pass 42 — first V4-A architecture.** A functional codec combined palette initialization, protected SKIP, procedural residual surfaces, regional transforms, shared XZ and source-resolution detail. It decoded deterministically and raised the quality ceiling, but had low-rate and runtime failures. Coast intermediate encode was about 34 s and fresh decode about 11.8 s; memory reached about 746 MiB in a large-image experiment. See [architecture](../research_pass42/ARCHITECTURE.md), [results](../research_pass42/V4_CODEC_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 43 — low-rate and decoder diagnosis.** V3 obtains low-rate efficiency from both compact generated-grid initialization and early coarse procedural residual patches. Coast V3 init: 1,783 B / MSE 156.15; V4-A init-only: 32,056 B / MSE 45.23. Coarse refinement was prototyped and decoder optimization measured separately; the correction did not recover useful broad competitiveness. See [diagnosis](../research_pass43/LOW_RATE_DIAGNOSIS.md), [profile](../research_pass43/DECODER_PROFILE.md), [results](../research_pass43/PASS43_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 44 — strict-budget competitive check.** V4-B files were much larger/slower on ordinary points. Strict-budget selective detail reduced MSE only 0.18–1.01% at 4.9–4.95% larger files on three images; no source-to-file integrated timing was available. A Coast point neared pinned AVIF q0 but was not a four-axis win. See [results](../research_pass44/PASS44_RESULTS.md), [budget results](../research_pass44/STRICT_BUDGET_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 45 — canonical V3.1 reconciliation.** Pinned the published ZIP, embedded Python source, model, presets and benchmark method. HF screenshot discrepancy remained unresolved; release executable was not run. See [selection](../research_pass45/CANONICAL_SELECTION.md), [metric validation](../research_pass45/METRIC_VALIDATION.md), [results](../research_pass45/PASS45_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 46 — source-resolution procedural strokes.** Hough-derived straight strokes used little packet space but had weak image-level utility: no Coast/Googlewiki stroke met the material gate; one Mario64 hit was subtle and lacked matched native point. No complete file was encoded. See [feasibility](../research_pass46/FEASIBILITY_RESULTS.md), [representation](../research_pass46/STRUCTURAL_REPRESENTATION.md), [results](../research_pass46/PASS46_RESULTS.md).
- **Pass 47 — native candidate-selection oracle.** Instrumented control was byte/pixel identical in six image/preset cases. Ten sampled states yielded 80 candidates; two material local alternatives appeared, both on Mario64 Balanced. The cross-image gate failed; no full-file continuations or selection rule were run. Local gains are not completed-file gains. See [control](../research_pass47/CANONICAL_CONTROL.md), [oracle](../research_pass47/CANDIDATE_ORACLE.md), [results](../research_pass47/PASS47_RESULTS.md).

## Technical findings to retain

**Worked:** V2.3/V3 low-resolution initialization is efficient on smooth/blurred backgrounds; early coarse procedural corrections are also important at low rates; exact-output optimizations (integral-image fusion, feature statistic reuse/histograms, and batched inverse transforms in isolated transform work) show that runtime gains can preserve pixels; sparse coefficient serialization reduces entropy cost when a transform payload is useful; V3.1's decoder is independent of model weights; Pass 47's instrumentation reproduced ordinary streams exactly.

**Failed or remained narrow:** sparse/dense source-resolution DCT refinement, fixed-grid predictive residual modes, expensive transform hybrids, multiscale residuals, hard-edge masks, decoder-aware target sharpening, patch omission, AV1 mode ordering and expanded transform search, AV1 AQ/chroma changes, V4-A palette initialization, V4-B coarse-plus-detail under strict budgets, geometric strokes, and expanded native patch-action search. These are separate experiments with different evidence and failure modes; see individual pass reports.

Repeated small changes within the existing representation have not produced a meaningful general completed-file improvement. Pass 47 does not prove global policy optimality. A future architecture proposal must identify information current streams cannot encode efficiently and test total-file cost, full reconstruction, perceptual quality and runtime before implementation.
